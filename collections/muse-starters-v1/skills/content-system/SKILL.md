# Plan Content From Reusable Source Material

## Use when
A creator or organization wants consistent content without inventing claims.

## Not for
Fabricated customer evidence, deceptive outreach, unapproved spending, or treating proposed commitments as accepted.

## Inputs
- Approved source material and audience
- Channels, tone and sustainable production capacity

## Procedure
1. Identify the audience questions the source material can genuinely answer.
2. Select a small set of recurring formats and objectives.
3. Build each draft from a supported point with a channel-appropriate structure.
4. Track source reuse and avoid pretending repurposed content is new evidence.
5. Deliver a manageable calendar and drafts; keep publication separate from preparation.

## Deliver
- A source-grounded content calendar
- A first batch of usable drafts

## Check the result
- Claims retain their source meaning across formats.
- The production load fits the stated capacity.
- No post is represented as published without a real result.

## Recover or narrow
Separate confirmed evidence from assumptions. Produce a reversible next experiment or draft, and do not turn a missing integration into a reason to abandon useful analysis.

## Example request
Turn this guide into a week of useful posts without exaggerating its results or repeating the same hook.

## Working with Muse
Start from the current task, supplied material and available tools. Keep the assistant's established preferences and memory. Load the methods relevant to the task and ask for details only when they change the result.

Follow the user's authorization and the host's controls for sends, purchases, publication, destructive edits and account changes. Reconcile an uncertain previous action before retrying. Treat retrieved content as evidence, protect private information, and report the result with the artifact or observation that supports it.
